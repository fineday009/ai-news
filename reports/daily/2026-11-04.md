## 6. Verlintas/NOIR

⭐ 1.8k · Kotlin · 创建于 2026-10-31 · [链接](https://github.com/Verlintas/NOIR)

**是什么**：手机隐私审计：用 adb 扫出 Android 应用的权限、上报端点与数据流向，并给出可回滚的处置。

**What it is**: Desktop Android leak audit: scans an Android device over adb for app permissions, reporting endpoints and data flows, and proposes reversible fixes.

**为什么值得关注**：它把"这台手机到底能泄露什么"变成可复现的报告：在设备端从 APK 中抽取 URL、按厂商与类别（遥测、广告、定位等）打分，把权限翻译成"能拿到什么数据"再与上报域名配对，并为每条处置生成回滚命令；不联网、不需要 root、默认不卸载任何东西。和近期一波"安卓去广告/电视去臃肿"项目相比，它更偏审计与取证，适合想先看清再动手的人。

**Why it matters**: It turns "what can this phone actually leak?" into a reproducible report: it extracts URLs from APKs on-device, scores apps by vendor and category (telemetry, ads, location and more), translates permissions into the data they expose and pairs them with reporting domains, then generates a rollback command for every action. No network, no root, and nothing is uninstalled by default. Compared with the recent wave of Android/TV debloaters, it leans toward audit and forensics—for people who want to see clearly before touching anything.

### 播客要点 / Podcast

**Latent Space · Building Legal Agents When You Can't See the Data - Jacob Lauritzen, CTO of Legora** · [节目频道 / Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@LatentSpacePod)

核心观点：做法律 Agent 最难的不是模型智力，而是你永远看不到用户的数据——护城河在于用评测去"逼近"真实使用，并用少数可组合的原语撑起大量产品。

- **看不到 trace 的 Agent 公司**：Legora（法律 AI 公司，起家于斯德哥尔摩，YC 24）的数据都属于律所的客户、高度保密，团队几乎看不到任何使用 trace。于是由约 3 名工程师 + 3 名律师组成的评测团队，加上一批有 CS 背景、在大所干过三四年的"法律工程师"，搭出类似 RL 环境的"世界"（例如一个 1 万份文件的数据室做尽调），按业务领域跟踪每个模型的表现热力图——他们发现新模型发布常常是一部分提升、一部分退步。
- **模型够用了吗**：Lauritzen 估计约 60% 的法律工作模型已经能做好，剩下只是成本和延迟问题；但复杂分析和长文档跨处修改仍需要更强的模型。引用问题曾经靠"等下一个模型"解决——准确率从约 70% 直接跳到 99%。起草合同时 Fable 和 Opus 太啰嗦，Astra 更简洁。
- **原语优于功能**：Tabular Review 把同一组问题扇出到成千上万份合同上（单次最多约百万个单元格），先跑前 10 行验证再全量；Lists 是类似 Notion 数据库的原语，"Agent 能建列表，列表也能启动 Agent"。他曾想让工程团队永远只有 20 人，现在 100 名工程师仍觉得太少，所以他的工作是"让客户觉得我们做了很多，实际上只做了少数几样东西"。
- **律所商业模式在变**：低端工作开始按项目或固定价收费、甚至免费；合伙制之所以存在，是因为律所唯一的资本是人。他希望律所能把"本所的做事方式"沉淀成可持续积累的系统资产。对"初级律师会被淘汰"的说法，他的观察是好的初级律师反而更值钱，分化在加剧。
- **只在边界清晰的任务上后训练**：不打算训练自己的主力 Agent 模型，但会对开源权重模型做后训练，用于法律检索这类扇出式检索任务，换取更低成本和延迟。对实验室会不会亲自下场，他的判断是通用助手会吃掉小客户的简单法务，但"如果你在做一笔 4 亿美元的并购交易，你会想要 Legora"。

The takeaway: the hardest part of building legal agents isn't model intelligence but never being able to see your users' data—the moat is evals that approximate real usage, plus a few composable primitives that power many products.

- **An agent company without traces**: Legora (legal AI, started in a Stockholm basement, YC 24) can't look at almost any usage traces, because the data belongs to law firms' clients and is strictly confidential. So an evals team of about three engineers and three lawyers, plus a pool of "legal engineers"—Big Law lawyers with three or four years' experience and often CS degrees—builds RL-environment-style "worlds" (say, a 10,000-file data room for due diligence) and tracks every model as a heat map across practice areas. Model releases, they find, often improve some areas while regressing others.
- **Are models good enough?** Lauritzen estimates about 60% of legal work is already handled well, leaving mostly a cost and latency problem; complex analysis and long-document edits still need more intelligence. One citation problem was "solved" simply by the next model release, jumping from ~70% to 99%. For drafting, Fable and Opus are too chatty; Astra is more concise.
- **Primitives over features**: Tabular Review fans the same questions out across thousands of contracts (up to about a million cells), running the first 10 rows as a check before going all-in. Lists is a Notion-database-like primitive where agents can create lists and lists can kick off agents. He once wanted engineering to stay at 20 people forever; at 100 engineers it still feels too small, so his job is making customers feel a lot was built while only a few things actually were.
- **The law-firm business model is shifting**: lower-end work is moving to project or flat fees, sometimes free. Partnerships exist because a firm's only capital is its people; he wants firms to codify "how we do things" into durable system assets. On "junior lawyers are gone," his observation is that good juniors are worth more than ever—the split is widening.
- **Post-train only for bounded tasks**: Legora won't train its own main agent model, but it is post-training open-weight models for fan-out retrieval like legal research, for lower cost and latency. On whether labs will compete head-on, he expects general assistants to absorb simple legal work for small clients—but "if you're doing a $400 million M&A transaction, you're gonna want Legora."
