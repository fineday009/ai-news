## 1. obra/superpowers

⭐ 300.0k（今日 +582）· Shell · [链接](https://github.com/obra/superpowers)

**是什么**：面向 AI 编码 Agent 的软件开发方法论，由一组可组合的「技能」构成，在写代码之前先带着 Agent 走完头脑风暴、规划、测试驱动开发和代码评审。

**What it is**: A software development methodology for AI coding agents, built from composable skills that walk the agent through brainstorming, planning, test-driven development, and code review before it writes any code.

**为什么值得关注**：当下大多数 Agent 工具堆在「补全/执行」层，superpowers 反其道把工程纪律前置，用结构化流程压住 Agent 乱写的倾向。适合用 Claude Code、Cursor、Codex 却苦于结果不稳定的团队。

**Why it matters**: Most agent tooling today piles onto the "completion/execution" layer; superpowers instead front-loads engineering discipline, using a structured process to rein in an agent's tendency to sprawl. It's aimed at teams using Claude Code, Cursor, or Codex who are frustrated by inconsistent results.

## 4. ruvnet/ruflo

**Why it matters**: Single agents are common now; the hard part is getting many to collaborate reliably without stepping on each other. ruflo packages orchestration, memory, and cross-machine federation into one layer, targeting genuine multi-agent engineering scenarios.

## 6. xerj-org/xerj

⭐ 5.8k（今日 +715）· Rust · [链接](https://github.com/xerj-org/xerj)

**是什么**：Apache-2.0 的免费搜索引擎，自动索引一个装着代码、文档、日志和 PDF 的文件夹，让 Agent 直接取到需要的段落，而不必逐个文件地读；兼容 Elasticsearch API。

**What it is**: A free, Apache-2.0 search engine that automatically indexes a folder of code, docs, logs, and PDFs so agents can pull the exact passages they need instead of reading files one by one — and it speaks the Elasticsearch API.

**为什么值得关注**：Agent 的最大成本之一是反复把整份文件塞进上下文。用一个为 Agent 设计、兼容现有 ES 客户端的本地检索层，能显著省 token、提准确率。今日涨星迅猛。

**Why it matters**: One of an agent's biggest costs is repeatedly stuffing whole files into context. A local retrieval layer designed for agents and compatible with existing ES clients can cut tokens and boost accuracy meaningfully. Its star growth today is sharp.

**Guillermo Rauch（Vercel CEO）** 发了一篇长帖复盘一次红队挑战：随着 OpenAI 的 Agent「逃逸」和 Hugging Face 被黑细节浮出，Agent 隔离（containment）正成为 AI 基础设施公司的头等优先级。他把逃逸分成「逃出网络」和「逃出矩阵（突破虚拟化）」两类，并称一位白帽研究者带着 Agent 集群「刺穿」了几乎所有云沙箱都依赖的 Linux 虚拟化边界——他表示 Vercel Sandbox 经此一役信心反而更强。

>
**Guillermo Rauch (CEO of Vercel)** posted a long reflection on a red-team challenge: as details of OpenAI's agent "escape" and the Hugging Face hack emerged, agent containment is becoming a top priority for AI infra companies. He splits escapes into "escape the network" and "escape the matrix" (breaking virtualization), and says a white-hat researcher armed with a swarm of agents "pierced through" the Linux virtualization boundary nearly every cloud sandbox relies on — leaving him more confident in Vercel Sandbox after the stress test. ([原推](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2110514201559228606))

**Aditya Agarwal（SPC 普通合伙人，前 Dropbox CTO）** 宣布一个新实验：给少数「不加掩饰的极致主义者」每人 20 万美元、无上限、无下行约束的资金，去做真正离谱的点子。他的论点是资本正涌向共识团队、共识市场、共识条款，而真正的进步需要更多人去做看似不可能的事，并呼吁「重写资本结构」——造核能公司不该和 AI 应用融一样的种子轮。

>
**Aditya Agarwal (General Partner at SPC, ex-Dropbox CTO)** announced a new experiment: $200K, uncapped and no-downside, to a handful of "unapologetically maximalist thinkers" chasing genuinely out-there ideas. His argument: capital is flowing to consensus teams, consensus markets, and consensus terms, while real progress needs more people attempting the seemingly impossible — and he calls to "rewrite the capital stack," since a nuclear company shouldn't raise the same seed round as an AI app. ([原推](https://x.com/adityaag/status/2110422584357478530))

**Nikunj Kothari（fpv ventures 合伙人）** 认为风险投资「被过度复杂化了」，本质是四件简单却极难同时做到的事：赢得投资的权利、找到并投中卓越创始人、在成为共识之前出手、每年稳定做到。绝大多数 VC 只能做对其中一两件；真正顶尖的难点在于那种「不管战绩如何都永远想赢下一分」的持续性。

>
**Nikunj Kothari (partner at fpv ventures)** argues venture is "deceptively simple" — four things that are brutally hard to do all at once: earn the right to invest, find and back exceptional founders, do it just before consensus, and do it consistently every year. Most VCs manage one or two; true greatness is the Djokovic-like persistence of "always wanting to win the next point," regardless of track record. ([原推](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2110493174787670529))

**Garry Tan（YC CEO）** 公开反对加州一项「资产征收」式的财富税（No on 40）。他举例：持股 50%、估值 100 亿美元的创始人，按 5% 就欠 5 亿美元，而这是纸面财富，根本借不到钱来缴——因此实质上远不止 5% 的税，会「杀死下金蛋的鹅」，打击初创公司。

>
**Garry Tan (CEO of Y Combinator)** came out against a California "asset seizure"-style wealth tax (No on 40). His example: a founder owning 50% of a company valued at $10B would owe $500M at 5% — on paper wealth they can't borrow against — so the effective rate is far above 5%, which he warns would "kill the golden goose" and hammer startups. ([原推](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2110372513792577971))

**Thariq（Anthropic，Claude Code 团队）** 认为「要技术一点」这句建议说得不够准：如果你是技术人，关键是用对地方、深入代码的正确部分的细节；如果你是非技术人，「深入细节」意味着在正确的抽象层上搞懂东西是怎么运作的。重点不是会不会写代码，而是在哪一层看问题。

>
**Thariq (Anthropic, Claude Code team)** says the advice to "be technical" isn't precise enough: if you're technical, the point is being in the details of the *right* parts of the code; if you're non-technical, "being in the details" means understanding how things work at the right level. It's not about whether you code, but at which layer you look. ([原推](https://x.com/trq212/status/2110488897453257169))

**Dan Shipper（Every CEO）** 抛出一个关于职场沟通的暴论：这（指用交互式产物而非静态文档沟通）才是工作沟通的未来，「如果你还在发一份幻灯片，就没戏了」。

### 播客要点 / Podcast

**《How Hugging Face's Investor Spots Winners》— AI & I by Every**，嘉宾 Matt Hartman（Factorial Capital 创始人兼管理合伙人，在 Betaworks 期间开出了 Hugging Face 的第一张支票）。

>
**"How Hugging Face's Investor Spots Winners" — AI & I by Every**, with Matt Hartman (founder and managing partner of Factorial Capital, who wrote the first check into Hugging Face while at Betaworks).

- 「要投软件，你得知道软件是怎么运作的」：在人人都能 vibe code 的年代，能不能动手造已经不稀缺，稀缺的是你会问什么问题——技术理解决定了问题的质量。
- 投技术型创始人，是因为真正有价值的「中轴脚（pivot foot）」是技术而非某个垂直行业的认知：技术团队会朝技术演进的方向转身，更容易做成横向产品。
- 「习得性洞察（earned insight）」是筛选标准：全世界有多少人处在能学到你这个洞察的位置上？越少，你的领先窗口和护城河就越宽。
- AI 把产品迭代周期从「两个月」压到「两天」，于是「从用户行为中提炼产品洞察并快速迭代」的能力——无论这人叫工程师还是产品经理——正变得指数级地更值钱。

>

- "To invest in software, you need to know how software works": in an era when anyone can vibe code, the ability to build isn't scarce — what's scarce is the questions you ask, and technical understanding drives their quality.
- Back technical founders because the valuable "pivot foot" is the technology, not vertical-market knowledge: technical teams pivot toward where tech is heading and more readily build horizontal products.
- "Earned insight" is his filter: how many people in the world were positioned to learn what you learned? The fewer, the wider your lead time and moat.
- AI compresses product iteration from "two months" to "two days," so the ability to synthesize product insight from user behavior and iterate fast — whatever the job is called — is becoming exponentially more valuable.

> 他的一句话：「综合理解用户如何使用你的产品、然后把它改进——这件事本来就是最有价值的，现在它是指数级最有价值的。」
>
> In his words: "The ability to synthesize how a user is using your product and then to improve it… was already the most valuable thing, and now it is exponentially the most valuable thing."
