### 🎧 播客 / Podcast

**No Priors —《Why Traditional Benchmarks Fail Modern AI Models》（嘉宾：OpenAI 研究科学家 Noam Brown）**

- 基准跑分被"误读"：GPT-5.5 在跑分表上只比 5.4 高几个百分点，被人质疑没多大进步；但那张表没有控制测试时算力（test-time compute）。5.5 其实"思考"效率高得多，一旦把思考时长拉齐，5.5 是实打实的大跨越。
- 模型不再很快"触顶"：以 GPT-3 时代的模型跑到性能平台期不难，但现在只要 scaffold 得当，5.5 这类模型能连续"思考"数周甚至数月才到平台——这意味着评测必须设定 token/成本/时间预算，或直接把性能画成随算力变化的曲线。
- 刷榜很容易也很误导：把一个模型跑 5 次取最优、或让裁判选最好的一条，分数就能显著上升——看着更强，控制算力后其实没变强；解法之一是保留私有、不公开的测试集。
- 安全评估存在"不便明说的真相"：各家的 preparedness/责任扩展框架大多诞生于"测试时算力还不重要"的 ChatGPT 时代，没有把"花多少钱评测"考虑进去；而如今"模型能做什么"本质上是钱的函数，危险能力的评测预算问题正在被回避。
- 推理进步很具体：在他做扑克 solver 的私人测评里，5.2 还会"煤气灯"式糊弄（声称在底池 100、弃牌只亏 92"差不多"），到 5.5 已能在轻度引导下零样本完成完整 solver；他预计一年内模型能一口气零样本写出"我的整篇博士论文"。

**No Priors — "Why Traditional Benchmarks Fail Modern AI Models" (guest: OpenAI Research Scientist Noam Brown)**

- Benchmark grids get misread: GPT-5.5 looked only a few points above 5.4 and drew skepticism, but the grid doesn't control for test-time compute. 5.5 is far more efficient at thinking; once you equalize thinking time, it's a substantial jump.
- Models no longer plateau quickly: pre-2022 models hit a performance ceiling fast, but with decent scaffolding, models like 5.5 can think productively for weeks or months before plateauing — so evals must set a token/cost/time budget, or plot performance as a function of compute.
- Benchmark-maxing is easy and misleading: run a model five times and take the best, or have a judge pick, and scores jump — looks better on paper, no better once you control for compute. One fix is keeping private, held-out test sets.
- An inconvenient truth in safety evals: most labs' preparedness / responsible-scaling frameworks were built in the ChatGPT era when test-time compute wasn't a big factor, so they don't account for evaluation budget — yet capability is now "a function of how much money you put into it," and the budget question for dangerous capabilities is being dodged.
- Reasoning progress is concrete: in his private poker-solver eval, 5.2 would "gaslight" him (claiming folding a $100 pot only loses "$92, close enough"), while 5.5 can zero-shot a full solver with gentle steering — he predicts that within a year a model could zero-shot "my entire PhD thesis" in one go.
