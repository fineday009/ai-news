### 播客要点 / Podcast

**Unsupervised Learning · Ep 90：AI 先驱 Jürgen Schmidhuber 谈当下 AI 现状 / AI Pioneer Jürgen Schmidhuber on the State of AI Today**

被《纽约时报》《福布斯》称为"AI 之父"的 Schmidhuber，对技术极度乐观，却对如今的模型公司相当悲观。几个要点：

Called the "father of AI" by the New York Times and Forbes, Schmidhuber is wildly optimistic on the technology yet deeply pessimistic on today's model companies. Key takeaways:

- **当前的算力资本开支被严重高估。** 算力每五年便宜约 10 倍，因此今天豪掷上万亿美元买 GPU 建数据中心的公司，五年内可能亏掉约 9000 亿美元——他预言会迎来一次股市"重新归一"式的崩盘（但不是文明崩溃）。原话："the guys who are investing a thousand billion dollars into GPUs for data centers today, within the next five years, they are going to lose $900 billion。"

- **The AI CapEx boom is massively overdone.** Compute gets ~10× cheaper every five years, so companies pouring a trillion dollars into GPUs and data centers today could lose ~$900B within five years — he foresees a stock-market "renormalization," though not a civilizational one. In his words: "the guys who are investing a thousand billion dollars into GPUs for data centers today, within the next five years, they are going to lose $900 billion."

- **递归自我改进不会成为护城河。** 开源模型紧追不舍，闭源无法涨价；而关键算法大多诞生于小型学术实验室——"everybody's cooking with the same water"，大公司很难靠一点点领先长期赚大钱。

- **Recursive self-improvement won't be a moat.** Open-source models follow closely, so closed labs can't raise prices; and most key algorithms come from small academic labs — "everybody's cooking with the same water." Big companies will struggle to stay very profitable off a small lead.

- **真正的 AGI 不能只待在屏幕后。** 机器人硬件仍远逊于人手（一只手里有数百万传感器、还能自愈），物理 AGI 可能还需数十年。而 AI 的下一步在于"人工科学家 / 人工好奇心"：像婴儿一样通过自身实验生成训练数据、自己提出好问题，从而摆脱对人类网络数据的强偏见。

- **Real AGI can't live only behind a screen.** Robot hardware is still far inferior to the human hand (millions of sensors, and it self-heals), so physical AGI may be decades away. The next step lies in "artificial scientists / artificial curiosity": systems that, like babies, generate their own training data through their own experiments and invent their own good questions — shedding today's heavy human bias.
