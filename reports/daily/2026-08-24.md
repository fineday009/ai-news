**Why it matters**: It folds chat, code review, CI/CD, and Git events into one event log, giving agents the same surface area and full auditability as human teammates. Rather than bolting agents onto an existing chat app, it rethinks the shared human-agent workspace at the protocol level—a rare substantive take on agent-collaboration infrastructure.

### 播客要点 / Podcast

**AI & I by Every —《Microsoft's Vision for an Internet Made for Agents》（嘉宾：微软 CTO Kevin Scott）**

要点：Kevin Scott 认为"智能体网络（agentic web）"需要一个像互联网那样开放的生态——Agent 要真正有用，就必须能代表你采取行动、调用工具、接入丰富的信息源，为此他在微软内部推动所有系统用统一协议（MCP）对话。他把 MCP 的安全模型视作社区可以共建的开放议题（Agent 需要身份与授权体系，以便"代表某人行事"并向用户请求权限），并认为"垂直封闭换安全 vs 开放换创新"是个假二分——用 AI 本身来做个性化安全代理，两者可以兼得。他最看重的是"无需许可的创新"：能不经守门人就把想法交到受益者手中。

Key points: Kevin Scott argues the "agentic web" needs an open ecosystem shaped like the Internet—for agents to be useful they must act on your behalf, use tools, and reach rich information sources, which is why he's pushing every Microsoft system to speak a standard protocol (MCP) to agents. He treats MCP's security model as an open problem the community can co-design (agents need identities and entitlements so they can act "on behalf of a person" and request permission from the user), and calls the "verticalized-for-security vs. open-for-innovation" split a false dichotomy—AI-based personal security agents can give you both. What excites him most is permissionless innovation: getting ideas to the people who benefit without gatekeepers.

金句 / Quote: "If agents are gonna be useful, they have to take action on your behalf… you need an ecosystem that looks a lot like the Internet."
